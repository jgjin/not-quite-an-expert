Hong Kong speaks 3 languages: Cantonese, Mandarin, and English. Almost every native Hong Konger speaks Cantonese; most speak Mandarin; and most speak English. So according to the Hong Konger I asked, everyday conversations often naturally mix between the 3 languages.

Note while Mandarin is much closer in structure to Cantonese than to English, Mandarin and Cantonese are mutually unintelligible. I can loosely understand Mandarin; I cannot understand Cantonese.

Out of courtesy or curiosity, I begin my conversations in ok Mandarin. However, if the conversation deviates from the expected scripts in my head, e.g. the convenience store cashier asks if I have a membership, I need to fall back to English. Upon my "sorry" I can see the other person switch not only languages, but also codes - English _for tourists_. It reminds me I am part of the East Asian diaspora, with its own joys of plenty and pains of not quite belonging.
