Growing up, I'd say "I listen to everything except rap and country". I now listen to rap. For example, I've been listening to [slayr's _Half Blood_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IiSpwFES-cE) a lot recently, and [Kendrick Lamar's _To Pimp A Butterfly_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qTmHuavOXNg) is one of my favorite albums ever. However, I still don't listen to mainstream country.

In Nashville, I visited the Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum, and it felt like a separate culture to me. Mainstream country music is part of _a_ culture, just not _my_ culture. In other words, [monoculture is dead](https://www.salon.com/2011/09/29/how_niches_killed_culture). I no longer listen to the radio in the car, instead to self-selected albums and artists on Spotify, including "alternative" country artists like [Adeem the Artist](https://genius.com/Adeem-the-artist-redneck-unread-hicks-lyrics).

[Yet monoculture rises again.](https://www.salon.com/2016/07/30/the_revenge_of_monoculture_the_internet_gave_us_more_choices_but_the_mainstream_won_anyway) [Kacey Musgraves won Album of the Year for _Golden Hour_](https://www.reddit.com/r/LetsTalkMusic/comments/aphxwv/lets_talk_grammy_album_of_the_year_winner_golden), and Beyoncé released a country[-ish](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2024-03-19/beyonce-cowboy-carter-album-not-country-album-cover) album _Cowboy Carter_ spotlighting the contributions of Black artists to country music. [Country music reached a significant portion of the _Billboard_ Hot 100 by fusing into pop and internalizing some hip-hop.](https://www.cantgetmuchhigher.com/p/country-music-always-comes-back)[^1] Now with much of music consumption driven by The Algorithm, we'll probably see [less genre and more "vibes", though we're still contending with what impact that has](https://www.publicbooks.org/spotify-vibes-algorithms).
[^1]: with some exceptions like Zach Bryan
