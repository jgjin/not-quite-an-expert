Given [the disproportionate negative impact of meat on the environment](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange/science/climate-issues/food), I try to reduce my consumption of meat. [While meat is important and necessary for many communities](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2016/10/what-if-the-whole-world-went-vegetarian), Americans like me could do with less meat, including [for health reasons](https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/whats-the-beef-with-red-meat). Still, when in Rome, eat as the Memphians do: barbecue.

I ordered myself a big ol' platter of meat from the Central BBQ next to the National Civil Rights Museum. Slow smoked with dry-rub spices over hickory and pecan, the Memphis-style ribs, alongside pulled pork, beef brisket, and greens, tasted good. However, I also tasted guilt for the reasons mentioned. As penance, I boxed the leftovers with sauce and utensils and gave them to a person on the street while heading to the National Civil Rights Museum.
