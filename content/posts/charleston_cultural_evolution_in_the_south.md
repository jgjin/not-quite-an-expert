In New Orleans, I took a tour with High Priest Robi, who described [Louisiana Voodoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Voodoo) as combining West and Central African tradition with Catholic beliefs under strict French (and Spanish) Catholic rule in close quarters. For example, Voodoo practitioners interpret Catholic saints as manifestations of [lwa (spirits)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwa). High Priest Robi also described [Louisiana Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Creole) as combining French with West and Central African languages, though many Louisiana Creole people speak English or French as everyday languages.

In Charleston, I took a tour about the local [Gullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullah) culture. The Gullah culture retains much more of the original West and Central African influence due to the relative isolation of the Gullah people on plantations in the Lowcountry. For example, the Gullah language retains much of the grammar of African languages, including gender-neutral pronouns. And the Gullah ring shout resembles African, not Christian, religious rituals.

Previously, [I described belief systems as experiencing evolution like organisms]({{< ref "idealized_religious_bodies.md" >}}). I'd extend that more broadly to culture. From their shared West and Central African origin, the Louisiana Creole and Gullah cultures evolved differently based on geography and intermingling, or not.
