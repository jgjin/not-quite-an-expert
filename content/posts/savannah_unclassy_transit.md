[Like with hotels]({{< ref "atlanta_hilton_honors.md" >}}), you could imagine transit options occupying relative tiers: planes luxury, trains midscale, and buses economy. However, [since the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978, competition has pushed flying to become much cheaper](https://www.travelandleisure.com/airlines-airports/history-of-flight-costs),[^1] pushing trains and buses more into the economy tier.
[^1]: to the point that airlines file for bankruptcy with surprising frequency, [most recently Spirit Airlines](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/02/nx-s1-5807933/spirit-airlines-ceases-operations-folds) 

The unclassiness of the bus was quite evident as I was waiting for the bus from Atlanta to Savannah. The bus station's announcer warned not to leave stuff unattended, or "it will get stolen", then complained the bus to Savannah hadn't arrived yet, so "stop asking me!" Another bus passenger laughed and commented how the bus had fallen, that it all seemed worse than when he last rode the bus 10, 15 years ago. Before I could structure a response, the bus to Savannah arrived and I lined up.

I think we still need the bus. Sure, the passenger beside only speaks Spanish, taking her call on speaker, and the passenger behind only speaks anger issues, hollering incoherently when the driver tells everyone not to eat food on _her_ bus. However, [especially in the wake of Spirit Airline's demise, many Americans can't afford to fly, so need to take the bus](https://www.wsj.com/lifestyle/travel/they-cant-fly-spirit-anymore-so-theyre-taking-the-bus-instead-175054af).[^2] Even for those who can afford to fly, the bus can provide greater convenience, e.g. the bus dropped me off right next to my hotel in Savannah.
[^2]: RIP Spirit Airlines - at least I used my voucher before you ceased operations
