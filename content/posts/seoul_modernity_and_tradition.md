(South) Korea is a place of modernity. [It has some of the fastest, cheapest internet in the world.](https://publicknowledge.org/why-does-south-korea-have-faster-internet-for-a-cheaper-price-tag) And the high-speed rail takes you across the country from Seoul to Busan in under 3 hours.

Korea is also a place of tradition. [It has the largest gender wage gap of all OECD countries because women are expected to break from their careers to take care of children.](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/centers-initiatives/institute-work-and-employment-research/understanding-south-koreas-gender-gaps-employment-and-wages) And with [the groom expected to supply the home to get married](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/kjd-special/why-engagements-that-dont-start-with-a-proposal-how-korean-couples-commit/12430466) in the face of [rising real estate prices](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20260723/explainer-why-real-estate-remains-one-of-koreas-most-sensitive-issues), [Korean investors are increasingly turning to highly levered investments to try to afford buying an apartment](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/i-couldnt-breathesouth-koreas-frenzied-stock-trading-exposes-margin-loan-risks-2026-07-20).

On the surface, modernity and tradition mix well - the pour over coffee looks especially Instagrammable against the backdrop of the renovated hanok cafe. However, under the surface, tradition must be adapted or dropped under the conditions of modernity.
