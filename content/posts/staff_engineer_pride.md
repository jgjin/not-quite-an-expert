# The advantages
In _Staff Engineer_, Will Larson describes the advantages of the Staff Engineer title over the previous Senior Engineer title:
> The three consistent advantages that generally come with a Staff‐plus title are:
> 1. allowing you to bypass informal gauges of seniority,
> 2. facilitating access to "the room,"
> 3. increas\[ing\] current and career compensation.
> 
> A potential fourth advantage is that some folks find that the title grants more agency to select the projects you work on, but others find that increase in agency is swallowed by a commensurate increase in accountability to the business.

# Analysis of the advantages
## allowing you to bypass informal gauges of seniority
Larson notes:
> While these gauges are believed to evaluate ideas objectively, their sheer informality becomes a broad vector of bias and often conflates confidence with competence. ... These informal gauges weren't mentioned by every Staff‐plus engineer I spoke with, but they were routinely mentioned by individuals who didn't conform to their company's stereotype of an experienced technologist.

In particular:
> [W]omen and minorities often do find they spend significantly less time and energy proving themselves once they attain a Staff‐plus title.

I appreciate that I _look_ like "an experienced technologist" (i.e. a nerd) to most people. For people like me, I find a loud, confident Senior Engineer can bypass those gauges about as well as a Staff Engineer.

## facilitating access to "the room"
Most software engineers I talk to[^1] enjoy the act of coding or building a system themselves. If access to "the room" comes with "a commensurate increase in accountability to the business" that separates me from that act, I'd prefer that act over "the room". I've had Staff-plus managers who clearly would've preferred that act, though were forced by the company into "the room" with a Staff-plus title.
[^1]: Note the selection bias of a loud Senior Engineer.

## increasing current and career compensation
If you wanted more money, should've joined earlier. Unless you have a concrete major expense you can't afford right now, I doubt the Staff Engineer title will materially change your lifestyle, except by separating you from the previously mentioned act because of the previously mentioned accountability.

# Current conclusion
I asked 2 coworker friends who were about to get promoted from Senior to Staff Engineer why they wanted the Staff Engineer title. Both of them acknowledged the increase in compensation wouldn't materially change their lifestyles, and honestly reported the Staff Engineer title would satisfy their pride, for now. I reflected that I don't need to push myself for the Staff Engineer promotion now, though I imagine knowing myself that I'll eventually want to.
