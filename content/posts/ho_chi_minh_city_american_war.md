I visited [the War Remnants Museum in Ho Chi Minh City](https://baotangchungtichchientranh.vn/en/exhibitions-and-events/permanent-exhibitions) to listen to Vietnam speak for itself.

The government of Vietnam calls the Vietnam War "Kháng chiến chống Mỹ, cứu nước", often translated and shortened to "the American War". And in the War Remnants Museum, the cruelties of the American War are on full display: [napalm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Terror_of_War), ["free-fire zones"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Lai_massacre), and [Agent Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_Orange#Use_in_the_Vietnam_War). Tens of thousands of American people died; millions of Vietnamese people died.

The pictures and stories in the museum cemented my notion that if there is such thing as a noble war, the last noble American war was World War II.
